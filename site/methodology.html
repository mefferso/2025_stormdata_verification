REPRODUCIBLE RESEARCH METHOD

Methods, provenance & QC

How the 2025 LIX SVR and FFW reporting-delay measures are constructed.

InterpretationThis study identifies candidate supporting LSRs and when their first matching archived text products appeared. It does not establish official warning verification, forecaster receipt, or the cause of later publication.
01 / SOURCE UNIVERSE

Warnings and reports

Initial storm-based SVR (SV.W) and FFW (FF.W) polygons come from the Iowa Environmental Mesonet (IEM) warning GIS archive for WFO LIX. Warnings are anchored to their original issuance in calendar year 2025. Tornado warnings are excluded. IEM VTEC event tracking numbers can be reused in one year, so the analysis identifies each warning by its VTEC number and original issuance timestamp. This avoids joining separate warnings weeks apart.

Report occurrence time, type, magnitude, county, coordinates, and remarks come from the IEM LSR GeoJSON archive. The source query spans late December 2024 through early January 2026 so year-edge warning associations are not silently lost. Source-category totals count unique reports whose event occurrence was in 2025: hail and thunderstorm wind gust/damage for the SVR-related category, and FLASH FLOOD for the FFW-related category. Tornado, waterspout, and unrelated LSR types do not qualify for this analysis.

Repeated GeoJSON representations with the same occurrence time, type, rounded coordinates, and county are counted as one LSR. All linked product IDs and remark versions are retained for audit. Records lacking coordinates remain in source totals when relevant, but cannot pass the spatial association screen.

02 / FIRST LSR PRODUCT

Historical issuance time

The main delay is earliest located raw LSR text-product issuance minus original warning issuance. The report's claimed weather occurrence time is used for matching, not as the publication timestamp. IEM GeoJSON's product_id may point to a later summary or correction. Its timestamp alone is therefore only a proxy.

The pipeline downloads monthly IEM LSRLIX raw text-product ZIP archives, starting January 2025 and continuing through the rebuild date. It parses each archived report row and matches it to GeoJSON by local event date/time, event type, county, and 0.01° coordinates. It selects the earliest matching raw product and retains the matched product IDs, issue times, text hashes, archive months, and ZIP entry numbers in raw_provenance.csv. ZIP entry numbers matter because distinct products sometimes share the same filename and issuance minute.

Archive-confirmed means the earliest exact match found in IEM's available text archive, not a guarantee that every historical NWS product was retained. If a potentially earlier product at the same event time/location has a conflicting event type, the record is marked as a possible correction and its first issuance is uncertain. If no exact raw match exists, the GeoJSON product time is shown explicitly as a proxy and is excluded from verified timing metrics. The same applies to missing or ambiguous timestamps. Later summary products never silently replace an earlier located raw product.

Reports issued before the warning retain a negative delay and a QC flag. They are excluded from the nonnegative post-warning distribution and denominator rather than clamped to zero. Products issued days after the event remain eligible. UTC timestamps and America/Chicago display times preserve cross-midnight and daylight-saving offsets.

03 / CANDIDATE SUPPORT

Conservative attribution

A report must have a usable point location, an occurrence within the applicable warning's validity period, and a point covered by its storm-based polygon. IEM's polygon end is used where supplied, including a shortened end relative to initial expiration. Initial warning polygons may still miss later boundary changes and local cancellation details; these require manual review.

For SVRs, hail at least 1.00 inch and thunderstorm gusts at least 58 mph qualify as automated candidates. Missing magnitude and qualitative thunderstorm wind damage require review. Non-severe magnitudes and tornado reports do not qualify. For FFWs, a FLASH FLOOD report requires a specific impact in the archived description, such as an inundated road, stranded vehicle, rescue, or affected structure. Generic county/parish flooding statements remain review records. These rules screen evidence; they do not certify an event or warning.

If exactly one eligible warning is a candidate for an LSR, that warning receives the report-level primary attribution. All other plausible links remain in associations.csv. If multiple candidate warnings overlap the report, none is chosen arbitrarily; the LSR enters an explicit overlap-review category and is withheld from primary timing totals. Reports with only qualitative or sparse evidence are also shown for manual review.

04 / COUNTS AND DENOMINATORS

What the numbers mean

Source LSRs count unique 2025 event-time reports of the relevant category, whether or not a warning matched. Associated LSRs count unique reports with one unambiguous candidate warning. Reliably timed associated LSRs are that subset with an archive-confirmed first product and a nonnegative warning-issuance-to-product delay. Unknown, proxy-only, correction-conflicted, and pre-warning times are shown separately and excluded from the timing denominator.

Within 8 hours means delay ≤8 hours; beyond 8 hours means >8 hours. Each percentage divides by reliably timed associated LSRs, overall or within SVR/FFW as labeled. Distribution intervals are 0–1, >1–3, >3–8, >8–12, >12–24, >24–48, >48–72, >72–96, and >96 hours. The >24/>48/>72/>96 threshold counts overlap; they must not be added together. The cumulative curve also uses the reliably timed LSR denominator.

Warning counts use distinct original warning episodes. A warning with multiple candidate reports counts once. Its first reliably timed candidate LSR determines its within-8 or after-8 classification. Warnings with candidates but no reliable timing are separate from those requiring evidence review and those with no identified candidate. “No candidate found” is not an official unverified count.

05 / LIMITATIONS

What this cannot establish

  • The IEM LSR and text archives are public historical sources, but coverage and report specificity can vary. “First located” does not prove an earlier lost or unarchived product never existed.
  • The report occurrence time may be estimated or revised. Published product time is neither when an event occurred nor when a forecaster first learned of it.
  • Automatically matched points and impact descriptions require expert verification against full warning text, local reports, and event context.
  • Initial warning polygons and IEM validity fields may not fully reconstruct every continuation, cancellation, or polygon change. QC flags identify known differences.
  • Multiple reports may describe one weather event; the dashboard counts unique LSR records, not necessarily unique severe-weather events.
  • Publication more than 8 hours later does not prove active follow-up, and lack of a candidate LSR does not prove a warning was unverified.
06 / REPRODUCIBILITY

Exports and build

The source repository contains ingestion code, offline tests, output validation, and a GitHub Actions deployment with a browser smoke test. The generated research files are results.json, reports.csv, warnings.csv, associations.csv, raw_provenance.csv, and summary.txt. Metadata records the raw text archive coverage through the rebuild date.
