Methodology & data quality

What this dashboard can—and cannot—tell us about delayed documentation.

Research cautionThis project measures consistency between warning polygons, LSR event times, and the timestamps associated with archived products. It cannot establish official warning verification, the time an office first learned of an event, or whether a forecaster sought follow-up.
01 / SOURCES

Archival sources & provenance

Warning polygons: Iowa Environmental Mesonet (IEM) VTEC/WWA GIS archive. Filter: LIX, severe thunderstorm (SV.W) and flash flood (FF.W), NEW storm-based polygons issued 2025. The IEM shapefile provides initial geometry, valid periods, event tracking numbers, and links to archived warning products. NEW polygons do not fully reconstruct every SVS continuation, cancellation, or changing polygon. A 2025 event is anchored to its initial warning issuance year.

Storm reports: IEM LSR GeoJSON archive, filtered to LIX in up-to-31-day occurrence-time chunks from December 29, 2024 through January 4, 2026. This surrounds the calendar year to include reports linked to year-edge warnings. IEM itself states that its LSR archive is neither exhaustive nor official.

Timestamp caution: The GeoJSON provides valid, the report's claimed event occurrence timestamp, and product_id, a source-product reference. We parse the leading 12 digits of the IEM product identifier as the archived text-product time. Importantly, the IEM GeoJSON SQL can prefer product_id_summary over product_id; this can refer to a later summary, rather than the very first LSR dissemination. Therefore, all delays are explicitly labeled product-linked proxies. They cannot reliably prove the first LSR issuance time, receipt time, or follow-up timing. When the product time is absent, the report stays visible as a data-quality issue and is excluded from delay metrics.

02 / SPATIAL & TEMPORAL MATCHING

Conservative screening rules

For an LSR to be associated with a warning, its event occurrence time must be within the warning's effective polygon window, and the report point must fall inside or on that polygon. Published product time does not decide whether the event occurred within the warning. The initial polygon archive is used; some polygon updates and cancellations may not be completely represented. No automatic spatial/temporal buffers are applied. Unlocated reports are not spatially matched.

WarningStrict candidate documentationManual review

If one LSR could correspond to multiple contemporaneous eligible warnings, each association is marked review-only, avoiding unsubstantiated support of every overlapping warning. Apparent duplicates across archive chunks are eliminated by a record fingerprint. Multiple corrections of the same event are retained but assigned a common event-group key for distinct-event counts; differing times, coordinates, types, or remarks may still require manual deduplication.

04 / KNOWN LIMITATIONS

What remains unresolved

First reporting time is not guaranteed: IEM GeoJSON's source product may point to a summary product. Research conclusions should use the wording “source-linked dissemination proxy,” not “first received report” or “follow-up success.” A stronger extension would separately parse each raw LSR text product and its issued time, then reconcile corrections to establish the earliest dissemination.

Archive coverage and polygons: IEM archives are incomplete or subject to correction. Initial warning polygons are used. Warnings that change valid time or area, and any canceled segment, require event-level reconstruction for precise verification-style work.

Timestamp uncertainty: LSR occurrence times can be estimated or rounded; report sources may have acquired the information earlier. The source's product timestamp is not the moment the LSR reached forecasters. Negative/implausible delays, missing geometries and missing timestamps are not made up.

Flash flood: “FLASH FLOOD” type and extensive remarks are not proof that official flash-flood verification criteria were met. Human examination of impacts, evidence, location, timing and warning criteria is needed.

One-to-many and review flags: Shared reports do not automatically document all overlapping warning events. Overlapping candidates are removed from strict counts and retained for review. Hail sizes and wind magnitudes use IEM's native report magnitude units; an unfamiliar or missing magnitude remains review-only.

Source completeness: Historical LSR records without lat/lon are present in the archive but cannot be associated spatially, and these are counted in source metadata rather than marked no event. Blank candidate-product timestamps generate QC flags instead of fabricated lags.

Operational use: This is an exploratory, automatically reconstructed dataset, not an official WFO verification system. Compare representative individual warnings with archived warning text, follow-up statements, event remarks, and local Storm Data before presenting findings as definitive.

05 / REPRODUCIBILITY

Rebuild & audit

The public GitHub repository contains processing code, automated tests and a manual-refresh GitHub Actions pipeline. Individual warning identifiers and report product references remain in exported CSVs. The workflow caches downloaded archive slices between runs. A failed download, no warnings, no reports, or absent product timestamps cause the pipeline to fail rather than publish fabricated results.

Files: warnings.csv, reports.csv, associations.csv, results.json, summary.txt.
